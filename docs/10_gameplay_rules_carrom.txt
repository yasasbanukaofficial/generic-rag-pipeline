BATTLEX PLATFORM - GAMEPLAY RULES & ENGINE: CARROM

GAME OVERVIEW AND BOARD SPECIFICATION
Carrom is a tabletop tabletop strike-and-pocket game popular across South Asia.
In BATTLEX, Carrom is modeled via an internal deterministic 2D physics engine
incorporating accurate friction, restitution, and collision mechanics.

Board Layout:
   Square wooden playing surface enclosed by wooden bumpers (frame).
   4 circular corner pockets with netting.
   4 Baseline zones parallel to each side with circular baseline circles (cut circles).
   Concentric circles at the center of the board (center circle) where carrom men are racked.

Equipment & Pieces:
   9 White Carrom Men (Light)
   9 Black Carrom Men (Dark)
   1 Red Queen (highest value piece)
   1 Striker (larger, heavier acrylic/smooth disc used to strike carrom men)

Seating & Game Variants:
   Supported Variants in Engine:
     ICF (International Carrom Federation Standard): 2 players (White vs Black).
     FFA (Free-For-All Points): 2 or 4 players competing for total points.


STRIKER POSITIONING AND SHOT LAUNCHING
Baseline Positioning:
   The striker must be positioned touching both lines of the player's baseline.
   The striker must either touch both baseline circles or completely cover a baseline circle.
   The striker cannot be positioned outside the baseline boundaries.

Striking Action & Physics Payload:
   The player launches the striker by specifying:
     Position (`pos`): Coordinate along the baseline.
     Angle (`angle`): Direction vector of the shot (in milliradians or fixed-point units).
     Force / Impulse (`force`): Striking power applied to the striker.
   Action payload submitted to server:
     ```json
     {
       "type": "strike",
       "payload": {
         "pos": 15000,
         "angle": 45000,
         "force": 80000
       }
     }
     ```
   Server-Authoritative Physics Simulation:
     The server executes a fixed-step 2D physics simulation (`physics_simulation.go`).
     Simulates striker travel, rebounds off wooden rails, elastic collisions with carrom men,
       piece-to-piece scattering, and pocket sinks.


SCORING, QUEEN AND COVER RULES
General Pocketing Rules:
   If a player pockets one of their own carrom men:
     They earn points and are awarded a continuation turn (they strike again).
   If a player fails to pocket any piece:
     The turn ends and passes to the next opponent.

The Red Queen Rules:
   The Red Queen is the most valuable piece on the board.
   Requisite: The Queen can be pocketed at any time, but it MUST be "Covered"!
   The Cover Rule:
     After pocketing the Queen, the player MUST pocket another carrom man of their
       own color either on the very same strike, or on their immediate next strike.
     If the player successfully covers the Queen:
       The Queen is officially claimed by the player and remains in the pocket.
     If the player fails to cover the Queen on the next strike:
       The Queen is Resotted (placed back into the center circle) and the turn ends!

Respotting Mechanics:
   When pieces must return to the board (due to an uncovered Queen, foul penalty,
     or piece knocked off the playing surface):
   Pieces are placed back in the center circle according to standard geometric respotting
     rules (`respot.go`). If the center circle is occupied, pieces are placed touching it
     along the opposite baseline axis.


FOULS AND PENALTIES
Common Fouls:
   Pocketing the Striker: If the striker falls into a pocket (scratch/due).
   Pocketing Opponent's Piece: If an opponent's piece is pocketed without pocketing own piece.
   Knocking a Piece Off the Board: If any piece flies out of the playing area.
   Illegal Striker Placement: Striking from outside the baseline.

Penalties:
   A player who commits a foul incurs a penalty (a "Due").
   One previously pocketed carrom man of their own color must be returned from the pocket
     and respotted in the center of the board.
   If the player has no pocketed pieces, the penalty is held as a debt and deducted
     as soon as they pocket their next piece.
   The turn ends immediately upon committing a foul.


VICTORY AND MATCH TERMINATION
In ICF standard match play, the player who clears all 9 of their designated pieces
  after the Queen has been covered wins the board.
In staked matches, winning follows the platform standard 90/10 payout structure.
If a player resigns or disconnects beyond grace, the match is awarded to the remaining player.
